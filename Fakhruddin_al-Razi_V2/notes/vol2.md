# Notes — Volume 2 (al-Juzʾ al-Thānī): tawḥīd and tanzīh

## L5600–5626 Tamhīd
"God is one" has two senses: (1) His essence is not composed of parts — which requires showing He is not spatial or in a direction;
(2) He is free of contrary and peer (ḍidd, nidd). Hence two sections (aqsām).

## Qism 1: God is free of spatiality (taḥayyuz) and direction (jiha)

### L5634–5710+ F1: a being neither body nor in a body is *not* self-evidently impossible
- Many claim every two existents are either one pervading the other or separated in one of six directions, and that denying this
  is self-evidently false. The great majority: it is not self-evidently impossible; it depends on proof; absent proof, suspend
  judgment — **"this is our view"**.
- (1) **Disagreement** shows it is not self-evident: philosophers posit intellects, souls, prime matter, and the "I" as non-spatial;
  among Muslims **Muʿammar b. ʿAbbād al-Sulamī** (Muʿtazilī), **Abū Sahl al-Nawbakhtī** and **Muḥammad b. al-Nuʿmān** (Imāmīs
  [= al-Shaykh al-Mufīd]), **Abū l-Qāsim al-Rāghib** and **Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī** (Sunnīs); most religions deny God is spatial,
  within or outside the world.
- (2) The division "spatial / in the spatial / neither" — reason finds the two-member version incomplete; and "the third is
  impossible" is not felt like the falsity of "one is double two".
- (3) **The universal "man"** is shared by individuals that differ in individuation → as such it has no particular shape or place;
  why then think it absurd that the Creator be free of sense and imagination? Objection: universals exist only in minds. Replies:
  (a) the universal is part of the individual's quiddity; a part of an existent exists; so man-qua-man exists in re and is other
  than position — "though the inquiry remains": being *other than* position ≠ existing *without* it; (b) at least reason does not
  find a non-spatial existent inconceivable — but reason judges humanity, if it exists in re, has a place. **"So this proof is not
  strong; Avicenna relied on it in *al-Ishārāt*."** (Editor quotes *Ishārāt*, namaṭ 4.)
- (4) While absorbed in a hard problem one knows oneself ("I judged …") while unaware of one's shape, position or size → an existent
  can be known without its spatial features.
- (5) The opponents are **the Karrāmiyya or the Ḥanābila**. The Karrāmiyya, asked whether a sensibly-pointable God is divisible
  (composite) or indivisible (tiny as an atom), answer "one, uncomposed, yet not small" — acceptable to neither sense, imagination
  nor reason; if they swallow that, they cannot call non-spatiality self-evidently false — they are "obstinate" …
  … The **Ḥanābila** (who even admit parts) concede God's essence differs from sensibles (no joining/separating, health/illness,
  eating, loneliness) and say "He differs from His creatures and cannot be compared" — so they themselves reject estimation
  (wahm) and imagination about God; accepting them in one place and rejecting them elsewhere is arbitrary.
- (6) Knowledge of God's acts and attributes is nearer than of His essence, yet even anthropomorphists accept acts and attributes
  that defy sense and imagination: creation of *bodies* (we only see changes of attributes), making without matter or time, acting
  without change in the agent, acting without gain or loss; knowing infinitely many things in detail at once, acting without tool or
  fatigue, hearing and seeing everything. A fortiori for the essence.
- (7) The soul's essence baffles reason though most evident; and dreams give access to the unseen when reason is suspended —
  against what estimation would predict.
- (8) Sight sees but not itself; imagination imagines but not itself → not every existent is sensible or imaginable.
- (9) ★ **Place and time exist yet are not in place or time.** *Place* = the void/space (khalāʾ, faḍāʾ) a body occupies — the
  mountain moves, the region doesn't; regions differ numerically and to pointing → existent (against the mutakallimūn's "directions
  are mental suppositions": the motion is real whether or not a mind exists); it is not in a further place (regress). *Time*
  exists (divisible into years, months, days, hours — pure nothing cannot be divided or compared) and is neither spatial (things
  nearer a body would be "nearer to time") nor an accident of the spatial. "If you study what we summarised on **void** and
  **duration** in this book, you will know **the truth is to affirm the void, and that duration is neither a body nor motion but
  an immaterial existent** — so an existent free of corporeality must be admitted. **This is the strongest argument here.**"
- (10) **Mujassim vs Dahrī cancel out**: the anthropomorphist says two existents must be one-in-the-other or separated by direction;
  the eternalist says two existents must be simultaneous or separated by finite/infinite duration (so God and world co-eternal, or
  God originated, or time eternal). Each calls the other's rule mere estimation → both fall → God is distinct from the world *not
  by direction* and prior to it *not by time*. Indeed a direction differs from other directions not by direction, and a time from
  other times not by time. ʿAlī: "He who made 'where' where …"; Aristotle: "acquire another nature".
- Opponents' formulations: God created the world in Himself (mixing with filth) / outside Himself (directional) / neither
  (unacceptable); "two existents must be …"; "what is in none of the six directions is non-existent" ("neither in the house nor
  outside it"). *Reply*: as a self-evidence claim, refuted by the ten arguments; as a proof, circular — "in Himself or outside" only
  applies if He is spatial.
- **Critique of Avicenna**: he granted the conviction exists but ascribed it to *wahm*, unreliable beyond sensibles. Razi: if that
  conviction is as strong as true self-evidents, telling them apart is either self-evident (then neither would be strongly held) or
  inferential (then self-evidents depend on proofs, which depend on self-evidents — circle); if weaker, it was never primary anyway.

## L5838–5852 F2: no existent need have a peer; lacking a peer ≠ non-existence
(1) Reason does not balk at a uniquely-qualified existent; (2) a thing's existence cannot depend on its like (mutual dependence →
self-dependence); (3) individuation is unique by nature. So "we cannot conceive what is neither joined to nor separate from the
world unless we find something like it" fails — God alone is such.

## L5856–5996 F3: God is not a body
- *Doxography of the mujassima*: (1) form — human (Muslim anthropomorphists: a young man; Jewish: an old man) or **a great light**;
  **Abū Maʿshar**: idolatry began from ancient anthropomorphism (God a great light, angels lesser lights → a great idol and smaller
  ones) → **idol-worship is a branch of tashbīh**; (2) motion and coming — some Karrāmiyya deny, some affirm, most Ḥanābila affirm;
  (3) light-theorists deny organs; most Ḥanābila affirm head, hand, foot; (4) all affirm "above" — touching the Throne, or separated
  by a finite or infinite distance; (5) all affirm He is finite below; disagreements about the other five directions; (6) in place
  by Himself or by an inhering "meaning" (cf. knowing by Himself or by knowledge); (7) attributes in every part or each in its own part.
  *(Editor's footnote: Torah on oneness; Maimonides' Guide on anthropomorphic passages.)*
- *Seven proofs*: (1) every spatial thing is composite (right ≠ left; hylomorphism; shared spatiality + individuation) and needs its
  place → possible. (2) Spatial → like all spatial things in quiddity (vol. 1 F15 argument) → (i) all bodies need a maker or none;
  (ii) His knowledge, power, divinity would be necessary for all bodies or need a specifier; (iii) eternal and originated bodies could
  swap; (iv) He could split, decay; (v) inner/outer parts swappable → needs a specifier. "Strong — though it entails the possibility of
  **rupture and coalescence of the spheres (kharq wa-iltiʾām)**, which philosophers deny." (3) Spatial → finite (finitude of
  dimensions) → could be bigger/smaller → possible. (4) Spatiality is a genus (→ genus + differentia → composite) or a species with
  individuals (individuation from the quiddity would restrict the species to one individual; from outside → caused). (5) Composite →
  simple parts whose right equals left → separable → union needs an external cause. (6) Attributes would be in one part (God
  atom-sized), or one attribute in many loci (unintelligible), or divided (knowledge is indivisible — proved in the soul volume), or
  a full set in each part (many gods). Objection: the same in man → many knowers. Philosophers: the knower is the soul, not the body;
  **al-Ashʿarī accepted that each part of man is knowing and living — "very far-fetched but not absurd; for God it is absurd (plural
  gods)".** (7) If motion is possible for Him, why not the sun, moon, Throne or Kursī as god (their only defects are composition,
  finitude, motion) — "pure unbelief"; if impossible, He'd be like a cripple; and they themselves have Him sitting on the Throne with
  feet on the Kursī, and descending to the lowest heaven.

## L6002–6008 F4: God is not a "substance" (jawhar)
Senses: (1) indivisible atom — all atoms alike, so one eternal atom = all eternal; (2) subject of accidents — the Necessary is necessary
in all His attributes, so changeless; (3) what combines with another into a third — presupposes spatiality; (4) Avicenna: "a quiddity
that, existing in re, is not in a subject" — applies only where existence ≠ quiddity; Avicenna says this is impossible for God since His
existence is His quiddity. "A purely verbal question."

## L6014–6110+ F5: God is not in a place or direction
- (1) If pointable, He is divisible (a body — refuted) or indivisible (atom-sized — nobody says so; then a mote could be God). Karrāmī
  reply "one, uncomposed, yet vast" — and "you compare unseen to seen without a common factor". Razi: not an analogy but a disjunction:
  either something lies above a given point or not; if the same thing were above and below, one thing would be in two places (then a
  mountain could be one atom). **The Ḥanābila, admitting composition, are worse off than the Karrāmiyya**; the Karrāmiyya say He is
  pointable, infinite (or finite but vast), yet one and indivisible — against reason. "What is not sensed is more contemptible than an
  atom" — smallness applies only to what has magnitude.
- (2) In a place → needs the place; place is an existent (they say He *must* be above, so places differ in nature; directions differ to
  pointing; an atom's starting-place ≠ destination); place doesn't need its occupant, the occupant needs place → God possible; place is
  measurable, hence composite, hence possible; and it would be a second eternal. *Objection*: "in a direction" only means distinct from
  the world. *Reply*: distinctness may mean difference of quiddity (granted — God differs from direction itself, not by another
  direction) or directional separation (which requires an existent place and dependence). Bodies needing places is fine; God, not.
- (3) Infinite in all directions (infinite dimensions impossible; the world inside God, mixed with filth; the same part above and below,
  or composite; and then the world's finitude could never be proved), infinite in some (same proofs; finite and infinite sides alike →
  could swap; or unlike → composite; and the infinite part joins the finite at a finite edge) …
  … finite on all sides: (i) could be slightly bigger or smaller → a specifier/chooser made Him so; (ii) His halves touch on one face
  though both faces are alike → separable; (iii) empty space above Him — God could create a world *above God*; (iv) bodies could be
  created adjoining Him → joining/separation → originated; (v) **if a finite, composite, movable thing could be God, nothing would
  exclude the sun, moon, stars — or the Throne itself — from divinity** (their only "defects" are those three). *Objection*: "you call
  Him infinite too". *Reply*: we mean He has no magnitude at all, not extension without end.
- (4) He is in His place necessarily or contingently. *Necessarily*: (i) all spatial things are alike → all would have to be there;
  (ii) the parts of pure void are alike ("void has no reality but being void") → He would be in every place at once; "above/below"
  exist only after the world is created; differences cannot come from void's nature, its locus (extension cannot inhere in another
  essence — void would then be body), or something in it (regress); (iii) like a cripple; (iv) then some bodies too might be
  necessarily located outside the world, and the proof of the origination of *all* bodies would fail; (v) that place would differ in
  quiddity, hence exist, hence He'd need it. *Contingently*: (i) motion/rest would be possible for Him, each needing a cause;
  (ii) His location would need a specifier → originated, and what cannot lack the originated is originated.
- (5) **The earth is a sphere** (a lunar eclipse is seen early in the night in the far East, late in the far West) → "above" for
  easterners is "below" for westerners → God would be below some people.
- (6) Inside the world (mixture) or outside: a finite void has a shape not from its simple nature → from a chooser → originated, so
  in eternity God was in no place (change); an infinite void is impossible, and any place has another above it (He'd be "below"); in
  all places → one-in-many or composite, and still "below".
- (7) Place is existent (dependence) or non-existent (cannot contain). Same for bodies? (a) place = abstract dimension/void, an
  existent that possible bodies may need; (b) if place = containing surface, the world as a whole is in no place, and God would be a
  composite body.
- (8) Pointable things are pointable per se (bodies) or derivatively (accidents) — neither fits God.
- (9) A vast pointable thing has parts (what faces the Throne's right ≠ what faces its left) → separable.
- (10) Above the Throne: touching (the Throne is composite → God composite; then why not the Throne be God?), at a finite distance
  (same; the world could rise and touch Him), or at an infinite distance (an infinite enclosed between two limits). *Objections*: one
  thing in all places touching all parts ("you forbid comparing unseen with seen"); composite but inseparable; infinite distance
  like His infinite temporal precedence while coexisting with now. *Replies*: one-in-many is self-evidently impossible (else the
  Throne could be one atom in many places, and be God); like parts are separable; He'd be bounded below, so the distance has two
  bounds; azal is not a definite time.
- (11) If someone reached His edge: could he enter (like air/water → divisible) or not (hard as rock)? → "a great mountain above the
  Throne" — far from reason.
- (12) **Inductive argument**: the more corporeal, the less active: earth only receives; water acts somewhat; air more ("life
  completes only with breath"); fire most among elements; the spheres, subtler still ("for most ancient philosophers the spheres are
  not solid but like subtle lights"), govern the elements → perfect power implies no corporeality. "Inductive, but gives strong opinion."

## L6294–6392 F6: the shubuhāt of those who affirm body and place
1. The world was created in Him or outside Him (→ separation by direction); "neither" is unintelligible.
2. Two existents: one pervades the other or they are separated in a direction; neither = non-existence ("not in the house nor outside").
3. **God knows particulars; a knower of bodily things must be a body**: imagine a square flanked by two equal squares — the two are
   distinguished in imagination only if their loci differ (same quiddity and concomitants; accidents differ only by receivers) →
   the perceiver is a divisible body. *Philosophers*: He is not a body, so He doesn't know particulars; *mujassima*: He knows
   particulars, so He is a body.
4. He is an essence with attributes; inherence = the attribute's being in the direction of its locus → He is in place.
   *Muʿtazila*: not in place → no attributes; *mujassima*: attributes → in place.
5. **A debate Razi witnessed between an anthropomorphist and a "unifier", which he summarised and expanded**: every two existents
   are adjacent or directionally separate; the ground of this in the visible world is not substancehood (substances can't pervade),
   accidenthood (accidents can't be separate), or origination (non-existence is no part of a cause; the Dahrī who thinks the heavens
   eternal still judges them adjacent/separate; origination is known by proof, this by necessity) → it is *existence*; God exists →
   the same holds of Him (needs univocity of existence).
6. **Fiṭra**: people of every religion raise eyes and hands upward in prayer.
7. **Scripture**: the Qurʾān says He is "on the Throne" seven times (7:54; 10:3; 13:2; 20:5; 25:59; 32:4; 57:4), "Subduer above His
   servants" (6:18), "to Him ascends the good word" (35:10), countless "sending down" and "to (ilā)" phrases — and **not one word
   negating direction**; hadiths without number; the Torah likewise. "Interpret them"? — if tanzīh were the true religion it would
   have been stated plainly at least once, and the rest could then be interpreted.
8. **Vision**: God can be seen; seeing requires facing → direction. Mushabbiha: vision → direction; Muʿtazila: no direction → no vision.
*(Editor's notes quote Maimonides, Guide I.35, on teaching the masses God is incorporeal; Raḥmatullāh al-Hindī's Iẓhār al-ḥaqq;
Ibn Taymiyya's al-Jawāb al-ṣaḥīḥ.)*

## L6396–6440+ F7: answers
- (1)(2): self-evidence refuted; as a proof, circular — **"the reliable answer"**. Avicenna's "it is wahm" answer again critiqued
  (the self-evidence-strength dilemma).
- (3): assumes knowing = a form of the known in the knower — already refuted (knowledge is a relation).
- (4): inherence ≠ following in place — else being-in-place (an attribute) would need a prior being-in-place → regress.
- (5): the premise is false: philosophers' intellects, celestial souls, rational souls, prime matter; the **Muʿtazila's wills and
  aversions "not in a substrate", and the "annihilation not in a substrate" (fanāʾ lā fī maḥall) that God creates to annihilate all
  bodies**; and **relations exist in re** (proved in the chapter on accidents) yet are neither in the body (half the father would
  bear half the fatherhood) nor separate (fatherhood would be a self-standing substance). Further: "either X or Y" expresses
  receptivity to division, which is privative (receptivity is privative, else regress), and privatives have no cause …
