## L252–280 Razi's opening
- "This is our book in divine science (al-ʿilm al-ilāhī), called in Greek *Bāthūlūjiyā*; arranged as a Muqaddima + books."
- Muqaddima has 4 fuṣūl: (1) this science is the noblest absolutely; (2) can human reason reach certainty (jazm/yaqīn)

## L284–360 Muqaddima F1: this science is the noblest (4 wujūh)
1. **Nobility of its object** (God's essence & attributes). God is most noble because He is: (i) free of any agent
   and any receptacle (fāʿil/qābil); (ii) absolutely single → no constituent part; (iii) the only necessary-by-itself,
   all else possible and needing Him; (iv) the possible needs its cause *both* at origination *and* in continuance
   (ḥāl al-baqāʾ) — and even its non-existence is caused ("the cause of non-existence is the non-existence of the cause");
   (v) existence is nobler than non-existence; everything else "considered in itself is found non-existent", He alone
   considered in Himself *is* the existent → He alone is *al-Ḥaqq*, all else *bāṭil* / pure perishing (Q 28:88).
   Principle: the nobler the known, the nobler the knowing.
2. **Need/benefit**: the end is felicity. Bodily pleasures are base (animals share and exceed us; they drag the soul
   from the world of holy spirits to bestiality; they are fleeting; all people instinctively revere the abstemious).
   Spiritual felicities are lasting; their head is knowing the First Principle; ignorance of it = eternal loss.
3. **Experiential**: the perfect man finds his joy (ibtihāj) grows exactly as his absorption in these cognitions grows (Q 13:28).
4. **Both worlds**: worldly order needs true sharīʿa, which needs knowing origin & return; otherworldly welfare needs
   knowing God, angels, books, messengers, Last Day. Its master stands at "the last rank of humanity, first of angelhood".

## L362–588 F2: can reason reach certainty here, or only "the more fitting" (al-awlā wa-l-akhlaq)?
Razi reports that "great sages" held the utmost here is *al-awlā wa-l-akhlaq*; decisive certainty may be impossible in
some questions. He lays out four arguments **for** that view, without rebuttal, closing "a sea without shore" — i.e. he
effectively endorses epistemic modesty about the divine *in detail*.
- **Ḥ1 (the self argument)**: one's own self is the most evident object (every knowing presupposes knowing that I
  know, hence knowing myself; the condition is prior to the conditioned). Yet what "I" denotes — the visible frame, a body
  inside it, an attribute of it, or an immaterial substance — is maximally hard. Same with place (Platonists:
  extended void-dimension, *buʿd*; Aristotelians: containing surface), time, and body (atoms vs. infinite divisibility:
  "nothing but perplexity and taking the more fitting"). A fortiori God.
  *Euclid excursus*: Euclid's methods construct only triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, 15-gon; heptagon & nonagon
  need conic sections (angle trisection), 11-/13-gons are beyond him → reason grasps "a little of the much" even in sensibles.
- **Ḥ2 (sight/insight)**: visibles are too faint (motes), too overpowering (the sun's disc, which looks dark at the
  centre with light pouring from its rim), or moderate. Intelligibles likewise: too faint (fine gradations of mixtures,
  alterations), too overpowering (holy substances, above all God — "seventy veils of light"; "glory to Him who veiled
  Himself from minds by the intensity of His manifestness"), moderate. Result: reason can affirm absolute perfection
  and deny every deficiency **in summary (ijmālan)**; detail (tafṣīl) is not within human reason.
- **Ḥ3 (limits of conception)**: all our concepts are (1) sensibles of the 5 senses, (2) inner states (pain, pleasure,
  hunger…), (3) primitive intellectual notions (existence, non-existence, unity, plurality, necessity, possibility,
  impossibility), (4) composites built by imagination (a sea of mercury) or intellect ("partner of God" from "partner" +
  "god"). Judgments are bounded by concepts. God's reality differs from all of these *in every respect* (partial
  agreement would make Him composite). So His reality is not conceivable to creatures; we only conceive perfection/
  deficiency in ourselves, abstract the absolute notion ("the absolute is part of the restricted"), and affirm it of Him
  stripped of creaturely concomitants. Only *ijmāl*.
- **Ḥ4 (direction of inference)**: one moves cause→effect, equal→equal, or effect→cause; only the last is available.
  The human soul is the lowest of the immaterial beings; it ascends cause by cause (Q 53:42; 42:53; God is "the First"
  on the descent, "the Last" on the ascent, Q 57:3). Most souls stay in sensibles; few reach the imaginal; fewer the
  intelligible; most of those dissolve in the lights of the holy spirits — except one rising "from the ginger of Mars
  to the salsabīl of Jupiter to the camphor of Saturn" (astral reading of Q 76) to the holy presence; "a pure drink"
  (Q 76:21) = purification from contingency onto "the threshold of necessity". Q 34:13.

## L590–712 F3: one path to these cognitions or more? → two
**(a) Naẓar/istidlāl** (the "divine sages"): contingency or origination of sensibles + need of the possible for a
preponderator (murajjiḥ) / of the originated for an originator + impossibility of dawr & tasalsul → an eternal
necessary existent.
- **Critique of Avicenna's "way" in *al-Ishārāt*** (proof from existence qua existence, which Avicenna preferred over
  proof from the contingency of others): Razi says it is *not strong*: (1) its "if possible, it needs the necessary"
  branch is itself the inference from a possible's need for a preponderator — no independent route; (2) even granted, it
  leaves open whether the necessary existent is *these bodies*; one must still prove bodies are possible-in-themselves.
  Hence knowledge of the Necessary comes only via examining sensibles.
**(b) Riyāḍa/mujāhada** (spiritual discipline): purifying the heart of all but God, constant dhikr "with the tongue of
the body and of the spirit" → light, unveilings. Three cautions (maqāmāt):
1. **Human rational souls differ in quiddity** (some luminous/divine, some dark) — "argued at length in *Kitāb al-Nafs*"
   (= vol. 7). *Mine analogy*: most mountains lack ores; of those that have them base ores dominate; gold is rare and
   costly; a cave full of gold appears only across vast ages. So not all who practise attain, and none reaches an end
   (God's majesty is infinite). "Painted kohl is not natural kohl." Aristotle: "whoever would seek divine cognitions
   must acquire another nature (fiṭra ukhrā)" = strip intellect of sense, estimation, imagination.
2. The core is emptying the heart; the soul by nature loves the divine presence, the body's pleasures veil it.
3. **Riyāḍa without naẓar is dangerous**: one may take intermediate unveilings for the final goal; reasoning (which
   separates the impossible from the possible) protects. The ideal: perfect in reasoning + perfect in purification +
   innately apt soul. Aristotle (attrib.): "I drank and was not quenched; when I drank from this sea I was quenched for
   ever." "Who has not tasted does not know."

## L718–838 F4: the "knots" (maʿāqid) of the science — and the 10-part plan
Man asks about past, present, future.
- **Past** = know the First Principle's essence, attributes, and how things issued from Him; each has a "formidable knot":
  - *Essence*: if like anything we know (by sense, inner experience, or intellect) → it would be possible; if unlike
    everything → reason is left bewildered; what we can grasp is not Him, and what is Him we cannot grasp.
  - *Attributes* (two "very very fearsome" stations): (1) if other than the essence → God composite of many quiddities →
    possible; if identical → (a) we know the attributes while unaware of the essence, (b) each attribute has its own
    quiddity, so one thing would be many. (2) a single knowledge encompassing infinite universals and particulars *in
    detail*, "no affair distracting Him from another" — nothing in our experience lets us conceive it.
  - *Acts*: how can a determinate effect be ascribed to a cause that is not itself determined? Unless a will, a change of
    time, an interest, or removal of an obstacle arises, nothing becomes an agent after not being one. Creationists must
    solve this; eternalists *think* they escape it, but forms/accidents do come and go, and the chain ends at a changeless
    necessary being → "the difficulty returns".
- **Present**: which beliefs and acts lead to the great felicity.
- **Future**: that there is a maʿād — spiritual only, bodily only, or both — and its states.
- **Ten parts of divine science** (by induction): (1) proofs of a necessary existent; (2) negative attributes of *jalāl*
  (absolute singleness, free of all multiplicity → no contrary/peer, no body/space/place); (3) attributes of *ikrām*
  (knowledge, power, will, mercy); (4) His acts: (a) is He perpetually agent/generous? (b) how acts issue from Him (survey of
  all doctrines with their weak proofs and objections) (c) qaḍāʾ & qadar; (5) "His word" in creating the upper and lower
  worlds, so far as reason can, noting that the essence of this is inaccessible — incl. whether the corporeal world is one;
  (6) ranks of holy spirits, spiritual & cherubic angels; (7) place & time; (8) prophecy (doctrines, supports, objections);
  (9) acquiring virtues (ethics); (10) spiritual & bodily maʿād. *Editor*: (9) and (10) were never written.

## L840–858 Vol. 1 Tamhīd: three sections
(1) decisive, certain proofs; (2) strong *persuasive* (iqnāʿī) proofs; (3) on necessity, existence, individuation, quiddity etc.

## L860–880 Q1 Muqaddima: the six routes
To prove an existent that neither sense nor innate reason gives us, the only way: show that the things we do perceive
*need*, in their existence or in some attribute, an absent existent. The ground of need is **imkān**, **ḥudūth**, or both;
applied to **essences** (dhawāt) or **attributes** (ṣifāt) → six routes: possibility of essences; possibility of attributes;
origination of essences; origination of attributes; both in essences; both in attributes.

## L886–914 Q1 F1: the "well-known" philosophers' argument and its six premises
Something exists; each existent's reality either excludes non-existence (necessary) or not (possible); a possible needs a
preponderator; if that is possible, regress or circle, both absurd → a necessary existent. Rests on six premises:
(1) the possible's sides are not preponderated without a preponderator; (2) whether that need holds at origination or in
continuance; (3) the preponderator must exist; (4) it must exist *when* the effect exists; (5) circularity is false;
(6) infinite regress is false. Then one must show the sensible world is not itself necessary. One faṣl per premise.

## L918–1156 Q1 F2: is "the possible needs a preponderator" self-evident (badīhī) or demonstrated?
**Case for self-evidence**: people seek a cause for every event; a voice → a person present; a building → a builder;
a child shouts "who took it / who put it here?"; a beast flees at a snake's hiss. Even infants and animals move from effect
to cause.
**Objections (stated at full strength)**:
- *Q1*: those cases show the *originated* needs a cause, not the *possible*. "Existence and non-existence being equal, one
  cannot prevail without a preponderator" seems evident only because "prevail" connotes *coming-to-be*; strip away
  origination (a possible thing *in its continuance*) and reason does not judge it needs a cause — the estimative faculty
  rather thinks the enduring thing persists by itself until a destroyer comes.
- *Q2*: not everyone agrees: (i) the majority (creationists) say God became an agent after not being one, and that the
  renewal of agency has no cause; (ii) the man fleeing a lion with two equal roads, or offered two equal cups, chooses
  one "without a preponderator" (widely held); (iii) many mutakallimūn hold that **essences are equal qua essences**
  (dhawāt mutasāwiya) — "essence" divides into necessary/possible/immaterial/material, and the belief "it is an essence"
  survives changing those beliefs — so each essence's having its distinguishing feature, if caused by something further,
  regresses; hence specification without a preponderator.
- *Q3*: common people equally insist a building needs prior matter, that things arise in a definite time and place, and
  that a building needs a *human* builder — all of which mutakallimūn reject (God can create directly, or an angel or jinn
  may build). So common consent proves nothing, and "the originated needs an originator" rests only on consent.
- *Q4*: "one is half of two" is felt more strongly than "the possible needs a preponderator"; a difference in strength
  means the weaker admits its contrary → it is *ẓann*, not certainty. Sub-replies: (a) denying the felt difference is
  stubbornness that would wreck all self-evidence; (b) one counter-case suffices (modus tollens), a million confirming
  cases do not (affirming the consequent); (c) the difference is not in the concepts: [Razi's thesis] *no concept is
  acquired* (argued "at the start of logic"), and even with the concepts made fully explicit (possible = what admits
  existence and non-existence qua itself; need = dependence; cause = that by which a thing obtains) the gap remains;
  (d) a judgment either excludes its contrary fully (then no degrees) or not (then it is opinion).
- Plus: Ḥ2 large groups deny it; Ḥ3 if self-evident, the enduring possible would self-evidently need a cause, yet most
  say "producing what already exists is absurd".
**Razi's answers (defending self-evidence)**:
- To Q1, two routes: (1) suppose something eternal *and* necessary — we cannot judge it needs anything; so the ground of
  need is ḥudūth, imkān, or both; ḥudūth cannot be cause, part of cause or condition (separate faṣl promised) → **imkān is
  the cause of need**. (2) Directly: possible = equal relation to existence and non-existence; reason evidently judges one
  side cannot prevail without something external; the denier "leaves his reason with his tongue and returns to it in his heart".
- To the matter/time/place parallel: philosophers accept prior matter and time anyway; creationists answer that forms and
  accidents admittedly arise from pure nothing (so why not essences), and parts of time precede one another without another
  time (so non-existence can precede existence without time). But *nothing* is ever found arising without an agent.
- To the strength gap: "the true answer" — there is no gap once disputatious habit is removed: as long as equality holds,
  prevalence is impossible; if prevalence occurs, equality has gone and something has been added.
- To "many allow preponderance without preponderator": they don't *adopt* it; it is only *imputed* to them (ilzām ≠ iltizām).
- To the enduring possible: whoever conceives it as still equal-sided is compelled to admit its need; deniers think
  endurance makes it "more apt" for existence.

## L1168–1212 Q1 F3: the case that the premise is *demonstrable*
- **Avicenna is "inconsistent" (muḍṭarib)** here: in long and short works alike he claims to *prove* it, makes long
  irrelevant divisions, then at the decisive point claims self-evidence.
- **Abū l-Ḥusayn al-Baṣrī** (Muʿtazilī, *al-Taṣaffuḥ*): preponderance without preponderator = preponderance while equal =
  contradiction. Razi: no contradiction — "possible" = its quiddity does not *require* preponderance; the contradictory is
  "it requires"; preponderance occurring *neither by itself nor by another* is not that. Baṣrī assumed "not by another ⇒ by
  itself", which presupposes the thesis → either abandoning proof for intuition or circular.
- Razi's own supporting arguments: (1) need of the originated is self-evident; its ground is ḥudūth, imkān or both;
  ḥudūth is neither cause, part, nor condition → **imkān**. (2) Equality excludes prevalence; prevalence arises after not
  being → a positive attribute → needs an existent subject; that subject cannot be the possible itself (prevalence precedes
  its existence in rank; a locus precedes what inheres) → something else in which prevalence obtains = the agent.
  (3) If prevalence were self-sufficient it would be so everywhere ("the requirements of quiddities do not change"), yet
  writing on this paper evidently depends on a writer. (4) *On the premise of eternal duration (qidam al-mudda)*: each
  part of duration is originated; its prior non-existence's "being prior" is something over and above non-existence
  (earlier and later non-existence share non-existence, differ in priority) → an existent; it too is originated → a
  "before" before every "before" → duration beginningless. But it is composed of possible parts → possible; if it existed
  by mere chance it could fail, yet its cutting-off is impossible → it is **necessary through its cause**. So possibles need
  causes; generalise.

## L1216–1590+ Q1 F4: the shubuhāt of those who say a possible's prevalence needs no preponderator
(stated forcefully; answers come in F5)
1. **Enduring things**: if possibles need causes, the enduring thing needs one in its continuance; but a cause acting then
   either produces what already exists (taḥṣīl al-ḥāṣil) or something new (then it acts only on the originated). "Its
   continuance" is either the thing itself (already there) or an added originated state. The "priority" (awlawiyya) escape
   fails: if present at origination, the thing was self-sufficient then; if it arose later, it needs a cause.
2. **When does a cause act?** while the effect exists (producing the existent) or while it is non-existent (it is still
   nothing) — no third state → causation impossible. "It makes it exist at instant 2 from instant 1" — did anything issue at
   instant 1? If not, no causing; if so, the effect existed then.
3. **What is "need"?** Not the thing itself (we know heaven and earth while ignorant of their need; body is self-standing,
   need is relational; "body needs another" informs, "body is body" doesn't). Not something else: not privative (its
   contradictory "not needing" is privative), not positive (it would be necessary — impossible for an attribute of a
   possible — or possible, needing a further need → regress; and its bearer is existent [then making the made] or
   non-existent [pure nothing bearing an existent attribute]).
4. **Prior and posterior**: what needs must be prior in rank to its need (subject before attribute, relata before
   relation) yet posterior to it (need → agent's influence → being). "Quiddity needs, existence is what is made" fails:
   quiddity qua itself either needs (same problem) or not (then only existence, or quiddity's being qualified by it, needs).
5. **The jaʿl problem**: what the agent makes cannot be (a) the quiddity (if it were by another it would vanish with the
   other, but black cannot become not-black; and possibility is a relation between a quiddity and something else — "black
   can be black" is absurd); (b) existence (it is itself a quiddity — same arguments); (c) the quiddity's being qualified
   by existence (not an extra thing, else regress; if not positive it cannot be an effect; if positive it has its own
   quiddity and existence → return). So the existing quiddity needs nothing.
6. **Simple/composite**: possibility does not apply to simples ("black can be black"); composites are made of simples that
   need nothing and follow automatically from them; the "form of composition" is just another part.
7. **What is causing (taʾthīr) itself?** Not the cause's essence (we can conceive the Necessary and the world and still
   doubt one caused the other; a relation depends on both relata; fire and water share "being a cause" while differing in
   essence; "God's power is God's power" vs "God's power causes the world"). Not the effect's essence (power is described by
   causing, the world is not an attribute of power; "the world exists by God's making it" would become "by itself"; causing
   is in the category of relation, *muḍāf*). A third thing: not privative; if positive, either merely mental (then the
   judgment "x causes y" is false → no causation, or true → it is real) or real — a self-standing substance (no: it is an
   attribute, relational, and its own causing regresses) or an attribute (it needs a cause → regress; and here even an
   allowed regress is self-defeating — nothing would ever *touch* anything, a third always intervening).
   - *Counter*: the same division would disprove "this house exists at this hour" or "this body is in this place".
   - *Reply (for the sceptic)*: the division is exhaustive by negation/affirmation and each limb is refuted decisively;
     a proof sound in form and matter that may still be false would destroy trust in *all* proofs, including yours for the
     Necessary Being. **Razi: "this is in truth the strongest of the shubuhāt."**
8. **Non-existents**: special possibility relates equally to both sides; if it grounds need for existence it grounds need
   for non-existence; but continuing non-existence is pure negation and enduring — neither can be an effect.
9. **Free will**: if prevalence needs a preponderator, man is not free: able to refrain or not; if able, the act's prevalence
   needs a preponderator, from him (regress) or from another; with it the act is necessary (no freedom) or still merely
   possible (refuted "by six certain ways in the qaḍāʾ wa-qadar question"). Yet we self-evidently know the difference
   between voluntary motion and a palsied tremor, between the thrown stone and the thrower (praise/blame).
10. **Equal options**: fleeing from a lion with two equal roads, two equal cups, two equal loaves. "His choosing is the
    preponderator" — if he could not prefer the other he is a *mūjib*, not a chooser; if his preferring needs a
    preponderator, same; otherwise prevalence without preponderator.
11. **Need destroys possibility**: with the cause → necessary; without → impossible; no middle. Replies ("possible in
    itself, ignoring the cause"; "necessary now, possible for the future") rebutted: if quiddity = existence, existence
    excludes non-existence; if not, possibility must attach to quiddity (which is necessarily itself), existence (excludes
    non-existence), or their conjunction (same split); "future possibility" either becomes present (same problem) or is
    possibility now of what is impossible now.
12. **Timing**: the cause with all its conditions either pre-existed the effect (then the effect's occurring at this time
    is prevalence without preponderator — unless the arrival of the time is a condition, contradicting "all conditions
    present") or arose now (same question → regress/circle).
13. **Necessitation runs backward**: the issuing was either possible (prevalence without cause) or necessary (the effect is
    a concomitant of the cause; removing the concomitant removes the concomitated → the perishing of any accident would
    remove the Necessary Being). Replies ("the cause was removed first"; "a condition lapsed") fail: we see accidents
    perish; lapsing conditions give an infinite chain, simultaneous (actual infinite) or successive (then a present thing
    can be caused by something past and gone — and then one can no longer infer a Necessary Being from possibles).
14. **Any world-cause**: if necessitating, then why *this* point as the celestial pole, each star in its place, the
    thinness of parts of the complementary orbs, the world's position in infinite void (for those who posit it)? If
    choosing, why *this* moment of creation? On every hypothesis prevalence without preponderator.
15. **Qua cause or not?** A cause acts either *qua* its being-a-cause (a relation, hence posterior to both relata — yet the
    effect exists through it → prior and posterior) or not qua being-a-cause (contradiction).
16. **Necessitation before existence**: the effect must become necessary-from-the-cause before it exists; but that
    necessity is an attribute of its existence (posterior) → prior and posterior.
17. **Possibility as cause of causing**: a cause can act only on what is possible and needy; so possibility/need would be the
    cause of the cause's causing → (a) something other than the Necessary would affect the Necessary; (b) possibility and
    need are privative → non-existence causing existence.
18. **Causality is originated**: the cause becomes actually-causing only when the effect exists; that new causality is due
    to the effect (circle), to something else (regress), or to nothing (an originated thing without a cause).

## L1636–1758 Q1 F5: Razi's answers to the 18 shubuhāt
1. Enduring thing: "making the made" — if it means a *second* occurrence, not entailed; if it means **this very existence
   obtains by the cause's influence, "this is true and correct with us"** → the possible needs its cause in continuance.
2. The cause acts *while* the effect exists; "producing the existent" in the sense that this existence is due to the cause
   is "our doctrine besides which there is no truth".
3. Need may be privative. Their rule ("the contradictory of a negative is positive") proves too much: "non-existence is
   contrary to existence" would be positive (since "not contrary" is negative) → non-existence would bear an existent
   attribute → be existent. (Tu quoque used repeatedly.)
4. Even if need is debatable, **origination after non-existence is known by sense** (light after dark, heat after cold);
   the same prior/posterior puzzle applies to "being originated", and it does not refute origination.
5. The quiddity/existence/qualification trilemma would equally prove that air never lightens after dark and nobody stands
   after sitting.
6. The needy can be simple: **"black became black through the agent; substance became substance through the agent"**
   (Razi affirms the *making of quiddities*). Possibility = "not impossible to persist, not impossible to cease"; to object
   that this is relational is "clinging to mere wording".
7. The same division would refute things known by necessity → it contains a false premise; the counter-cases were offered to
   show this, not conceded as sound.
8. Possibility grounds the need of the possible's *existence*; or, if of both sides, the cause of non-existence is the
   non-existence of the cause; "causality is positive" fails by the tu quoque in (3).
9. **Free will**: "Why not say man is *compelled in his choosing*: God creates the choice in him, and the choice
   necessitates the act (or is part/condition of what necessitates it)?" Objection "then jabr, and command, prohibition,
   reward and punishment are void" → not conceded: **for the philosophers reward and punishment are necessary consequences
   of prior deeds** (to be treated in the maʿād chapters).
10. The fugitive does have a preponderator: his moving one way and his *will* for that motion; that will does not come from
    a further will (regress) but **"arises in his heart from higher (celestial) causes whose details we cannot know"**.
11. The "need destroys possibility" argument would equally destroy the possibility of any existent possible thing (its
    existence excludes its non-existence) — whatever excuses that, excuses this.
12. The agent's essence pre-existed; the lapse of the previous event is the *condition* for the emanation of the next —
    "as the philosophers hold".
13. The effect is a follower: when it is removed the cause is (logically) removed, but the effect's removal does not *cause*
    the cause's removal; an effect may lapse because a *state* of the cause (a condition) lapsed — philosophers: each event
    from the eternal cause is conditioned on the lapse of a prior one, without first.
14. The pole is fixed by the specific motion; the motion may be due to the specific celestial matter being receptive only
    to that motion — "on the philosopher's view" (the time-of-creation case is not answered here).
15. It causes not qua being-a-cause but **by its specific essence**; the two relations ("cause of", "effect of") arise once
    the effect exists.
16. True, but the necessity is an attribute of the *cause* (it must become such that its effect must issue).
17. The effect's possibility/need is a **condition**, not a cause, of being affected.
18. If causality were an extra existent attribute → regress; **so causality is not an existent in re** (it is relational/
    mental).

## L1768–1928 Q1 F6: two further objections
**Objection A — "priority" (awlawiyya)**: a possible may have one side *more fitting* without reaching necessity, and so need no
cause. Supports: (1) "flowing" beings (time, motion, sound) — non-existence is more fitting for them, why not existence
for others?; (2) a cause awaiting a condition (weight → falling unless obstructed): more fitting, not necessary; (3) the
philosophers' division of possibles into frequent/rare/equal; earth tends to the centre "for the most part"; (4) a quiddity
requires either neither side (impossible), one side indeterminately (an indeterminate cannot exist), or one side
determinately while the other stays possible → awlawiyya; and awlawiyya ⇒ self-sufficiency.
**Objection B — "eternal things need no cause"**: even if the heavens are possible, if eternal and enduring they need no
cause (the enduring needs none) — so you must first prove origination, which collapses your proof into the mutakallimūn's.
Supports: making the made; a building outlasts the builder; henna's colour outlasts the henna; the arrow moves after the
throw; enduring non-existence would need a cause; we expect our house to be there when we return, and write letters to
absent friends — "the default is that a thing remains as it was".
**Razi's answers**:
- Against awlawiyya: (1) take the quiddity with all that grounds its "priority": if non-existence is then impossible, it is
  necessary; if possible, either it depends on a destroying cause (so the "priority" needed that cause's absence — not
  self-sufficient) or not (then the same priority coexists with existence and with non-existence, and which time gets which
  is unpreponderated). (2) If prevalence is impossible under equality it is *more* impossible for the outweighed side;
  so the outweighed side is impossible and the preponderant side necessary — no middle "priority".
  Replies: (1) some hold every accident can endure (receptivity to existence is a concomitant of the quiddity); for those
  who deny it, "enduring" is an originated quality that is itself impossible. (2) If any condition is missing the cause
  cannot act; if all present it must. (3) "Frequent" = whose cause is present in most circumstances. (4) The quiddity
  requires "not being void of both", which is determinate.
- Against B: **possibility alone suffices for need; ḥudūth cannot be cause, part, or condition** — origination is a mode of
  existence, which depends on influence, which depends on need, which depends on its ground; so origination as ground
  would make a thing posterior to itself by several ranks. Building: the *moving* of bricks needs the builder; their
  *staying* is due to their own natures. The expectation of persistence is real, but it does not show the enduring needs no cause.

## L1932–1996 Q1 F7: on philosophical principles the proof needs the cause to *coexist* with the effect
Without this, each possible could rest on a prior possible that has perished, ad infinitum — and philosophers *accept* such
successive regress (every celestial revolution preceded by another). With coexistence, an infinite chain would be an
actually infinite simultaneous set, which they reject. **Avicenna in *al-Najāt* proves coexistence before proving the
Necessary; in *al-Ishārāt* and elsewhere he omits it — "the truth is what he did in *al-Najāt*".**
Proof: a cause either causes while it exists (but then, ex hypothesi, nothing issued) or after it has ceased (the
non-existent causes nothing); "it necessitated at t1 an effect at t2" fails by the same dilemma.
Opponents' 7 arguments and replies: (1) withness is essential (no) or temporal (only for movers) → *rests on time being a
concomitant of motion, which Razi rejects: we know God exists now along with every event*; (2) "making the made" — answered;
(3) the arrow → the thrower produces an *enduring force* in it, each phase of motion conditioned on the lapse of the
previous (same for falling bodies; some Muʿtazila call each phase an efficient cause, philosophers a preparatory cause);
(4) thought precedes knowledge → thought = knowing the two premises, which coexist with knowing the conclusion;
(6) "complete cause precedes" — temporally = begging the question; essentially = granted; (7) "I moved my hand, then the key
moved" — "clinging to words in rational straits"; read as essential priority.

## L2000–2010+ Q1 F8: a "better" formulation? (start)
Some say the objections arise only because necessary/possible were defined by (in)capacity for non-existence; define instead
necessary = *needs no cause*, possible = *needs a cause*, and the proof is trivial and immune. Razi begins: "rational
inquiries do not change with changes of wording" …
… even with "necessary = needs no cause", one may still ask why a cause-free being's quiddity could not be *equally receptive*
to existence and non-existence while its existence simply prevails (or prevails by mere "priority"). So certainty of a being
whose reality *cannot* accept non-existence still needs the earlier premises. **"Verbal moves do not repel rational
realities; they only shift the dispute from one station to another — of little use."**

## L2020–2094 Q1 F9: refuting circular causation (dawr)
- *Arg. 1 (priority)*: cause precedes effect; mutual causation → each precedes itself. Razi probes "precedence": temporal is
  impossible (and would ruin the proof); "essential/causal precedence" — defenders say it differs from temporal (finger and
  ring move together) and from causality itself (the cause must be complete first). Razi: the felt ordering *is* just
  causality; so the argument is "if each causes the other, each causes the other" — a tautology. If mutual causation is
  self-evidently impossible, no argument is needed; if not, this only swaps words: "it neither fattens nor satisfies hunger".
- *Arg. 2 (cause of cause)*: "the cause of a cause is a cause" — if *proximate* cause, self-contradictory; if *cause of the
  cause*, trivial.
- *Arg. 3 (need) — "the strongest"*: each needs the other; what needs what needs X needs X → each needs itself, absurd since
  (a) the needed is not needy towards the needer (same thing needy and self-sufficient in one respect), (b) need is a relation
  requiring two.
- Pro-dawr: matter/form and substance/accident need each other; correlatives depend on each other (a single cause of both is
  barred for philosophers by "from one only one"); causality/effecthood are correlative hence simultaneous, which excludes
  priority. Answers: **"we do not hold matter and form (hylomorphism)"**; body's essence requires being in *some* place;
  correlatives co-occur but don't *need* each other; correlation/simultaneity concern being-cause/being-effect, priority
  concerns their specific realities.

## L2102–2340 Q1 F10: refuting infinite regress (tasalsul) — seven proofs
- **B1 (the totality)**: given coexistence (F7), an infinite chain would exist all at once; the totality depends on possible
  units → possible → needs a cause: itself (= a possible needing nothing; needer/needed; relation needs two), a member (the
  cause of a whole causes each member, itself included → self-causation, circularity), or something outside → not possible →
  necessary.
  - *Objections*: (1) counter-instances: beginningless revolutions (philosophers); infinitely many surviving rational souls;
    endless events in the hereafter (mutakallimūn) and endless cycles (philosophers); God's infinite knowables and
    powerables; beginningless *possibility* of events (else a thing would switch from impossible-in-itself to possible);
    infinite numbers; **God knows, and knows that He knows, … infinitely — ordered by nature and all actual at once** ("a
    strong counter-instance"). The philosophers' "He is intellect, intellecter, intellected, all one" is **"pure ṭāmmāt
    (bombast)"**: by their own theory knowledge of black is a form equal in quiddity to black, and God's essence differs from
    black. "We proved at the start of this book that **knowledge is a relational state (ḥāla nisbiyya iḍāfiyya)**", so knowing
    one's knowing is a further relation. The mutakallimūn's "knowing that one knows = knowing" fails (one would know infinite
    ranks in detail; we can believe someone knows while doubting it; "Zayd knows black" ≠ "Zayd knows he knows black").
    Also infinite halvings → infinitely many ordered ratios. (2) Calling an infinite a "whole/totality" presupposes
    finitude → circular. (3) **A fourth option**: the whole is explained by each member's being caused by another ad
    infinitum — the proof refutes irrelevant options and omits the one at issue ("tamwīh").
  - *Answers*: (1) past revolutions never exist together; a non-existent totality cannot be judged needy; coexisting causes
    *do* form an existent totality (hence F7 was needed). Souls coexist but don't cause one another. Endless events,
    knowables, numbers, possibilities: their totality does not exist. Knowledge-of-knowledge ranks have a *first* (knowing the
    thing) though no last; we only claim causes need a first. (2) "Infinite" is predicated of the whole, so it can be called
    a whole. (3) The two notions — the units, and their each-resting-on-another — fail: the units *are* the whole (self-
    causation); the "restings" are accidents of the units, and an accident cannot cause its subject.
- **B2**: shorter: whole and each unit are possible; each possible needs a cause other than itself → the whole's cause is
  other than the whole and every unit → not possible → necessary.
- **B3**: what exists = the units + their relations; relations are iʿtibārī accidents depending on possible units → possible;
  everything in the chain, in essence and in aspect, is possible → needs an external cause.
- **B4 — the application argument (burhān al-taṭbīq)**: take the chain from the last effect upward, and the same chain minus ten;
  map unit to unit from our end; equality would make greater = lesser; the shortfall must appear at the far end → finite.
  (Objection that it would force philosophers to finite past events and finite souls: answered "in the chapter on finitude of
  dimensions".)
- **B5**: one possible with its infinite causes, another with its; two series > one; what is less is finite → contradiction.
- **B6 (middle/extreme)**: the last effect is only effect, a first cause only cause; an infinite chain is all *middles*; a
  middle by nature rests on an extreme; if it doesn't, it is self-sufficient, i.e. an extreme.
- **B7 (derived necessity)**: a possible exists only when necessitated by its cause; its necessity is *derivative*
  (tābiʿ); an infinite chain of derivatives without an original is impossible → something necessary by origin (aṣāla).

## L2352–2396 Q1 F11: a regress-free route — can a possible be a cause at all?
- *Ḥ1*: a possible's quiddity already necessitates its receptivity; causing too would give one quiddity two effects (against
  "from one only one"); if composite, the causing part is not the possible part.
- *Ḥ2*: possibility grounds need for a *determinate* cause (the indeterminate does not exist); possibility is one quiddity → all
  possibles need the *same* determinate cause; if that were possible it would need itself → necessary. Objection: possibility
  needs "a cause qua cause" (one notion over many causes). Reply: being-a-cause is a relational accident (itself caused), and a
  cause acts by its specific essence, not qua cause → possibility is ground of need for *that* essence → every possible rests
  on it. **Razi: "a good proof (burhān ḥasan) for the Necessary Being."**
- *Ḥ3*: every possible is composite (quiddity + existence); no composite causes (both parts independent → two sufficient
  causes; one → not composite; neither → after combination nothing new, or a new state → regress). **Razi: looks immune to
  counter-examples, "but full reflection shows it is not sound."**

## L2400–2430+ Q1 F12: the objection from daily events (start)
The proof needs 5 premises (possible needs preponderator; cause coexists; cause of an existent exists; no dawr; no tasalsul).
Daily events (ḥawādith yawmiyya) seem to force dropping one: uncaused (premise 1 falls); caused by a *prior* originated
thing that has gone (then every possible may rest on a perished predecessor, beginninglessly — the philosophers' own view —
and the Necessary cannot be proved); by a *coexisting* originated thing (dawr or tasalsul); by an *eternal* cause — if complete
from eternity, the timing is unpreponderated; if not, its completion is originated → regress again.
- **Two answers and their critique** (L2432–2480):
  - *Philosophers*: "a compelling, powerful problem, answerable by one word only, which works only on our view that every
    event is preceded by another without first": the eternal cause's emanation of each event is conditioned on the lapse
    of the previous one.
  - *Mutakallimūn*: it is hard only for philosophers who allow beginningless events; we deny them, so all events end at an
    eternal cause.
  - *The questioner replies*: philosophers' answer is weak — (1) the completeness dilemma still applies; (2) the eternal
    cause's *becoming* cause of the later event is new and needs a cause (prior event's non-existence / circle / regress);
    (3) the prior event lapses either by itself (then it could never have existed) or through the arrival of the later one
    → circle. Mutakallimūn's answer is weak — (1) the *possibility* of events either has a beginning (then before it events
    were impossible-in-themselves and became possible — absurd, and the switch-point is unpreponderated) or not (then
    beginningless events are not impossible); (2) a complete eternal agent with a non-eternal world = prevalence without
    preponderator; an incomplete one → its completion is originated → regress.
  - Final word: the philosophers say causality (muʾaththiriyya) is not an extra existent (else regress), so its "newness"
    needs no cause; the mutakallimūn's reply is deferred to the qidam/ḥudūth volume. **The "fearsome question" (al-suʾāl
    al-hāʾil) is left only partially answered.**

## L2490–2594 Q1 F13: doubts against a Necessary Being as such, with answers
1. It would be an *essence*; all essences are equal qua essence; equals share all rules → its distinguishing feature
   could lapse → possible. *Ans*: "being an essence" = being self-standing = not needing another — a **negative** notion
   that different realities share; different quiddities can share equal concomitants; by your logic all attributes would be
   equal too — whence any difference?
2. Necessity is its whole quiddity (no: necessity is known, His reality unknown; "the necessary exists" is uninformative,
   "this essence is necessary" informative; logicians treat necessity as a modality of the copula), a part (→ composite),
   or external (needs the quiddity → possible → its necessity is through a cause → regress). *Ans*: assumes necessity is
   positive — why not **privative**?
3. Existence = quiddity → "existent" is equivocal between Him and others (shown false); existence extra → caused by the
   quiddity → quiddity prior *in existence* to its existence. *Ans*: deferred to its own question (vol. 1 Q3).
4. Existence ≠ essence → composite. *Ans*: follows only if necessity is positive.
5. Permanence needs time (past/present/future); "we only say it does not accept non-existence" — words do not matter;
   what exists in no past, present or future is nothing. *Ans*: if permanence needed time, that time is permanent and needs
   another time → regress.

## L2602–2728 Q1 F14: the sensible world is not necessary in itself (8 arguments)
The proof gives *a* necessary being; one must still show the world is possible.
1. **Hylomorphism** (body = matter + form; composite → possible). Against "body is necessary through its parts":
   philosophers: matter and form are mutually inseparable *in existence*; matter cannot cause form (receiver ≠ agent), form
   cannot cause matter (it inheres in and needs it) → co-effects of a separate cause. *Razi's objections*: hylomorphism
   itself is disputed (see vol. 6); the inseparability proofs too; one simple thing can be receiver and agent; why not
   concomitant per se, like correlatives?; "co-effects of one cause" contradicts "from one only one"; and the philosophers'
   own "form is the *partner* of the cause" for matter is just "part-cause", which their argument also excludes.
2. Body's existence is other than its quiddity; such existence needs the quiddity → possible; it cannot be caused by the
   quiddity (it would precede itself in existence) → caused by another. (Whether a quiddity can cause its own existence:
   deferred to Q3.)
3. The Necessary is one; bodies are many (observed).
4. Bodies share corporeality, differ in individuation; neither entails the other → their union is caused from outside.
   (Assumes individuation is positive.)
5. Everything spatial is divisible (anti-atomist proofs) → composite → possible. "Deep inquiries in each premise."
6. Bodies' quiddities necessitate receptivity; also necessitating necessary existence = two effects of one (assumes both are
   positive and "from one only one").
7. Body cannot lack magnitudes and places; corporeality does not require any specific ones → body is inseparable from what
   needs an external cause → needs one.
8. **Mutakallimūn**: bodies are originated → possible.
"End of the proof from the possibility of essences."

## L2734–2830 Q1 F15: proof from possibility of *attributes* — first establish that bodies are alike in quiddity
- Difficulty (stated honestly): as attributes share "being attributes" yet differ (black/white/sweet/sour), bodies may
  share extension yet differ in specific quiddity (fire's corporeality vs earth's vs the sphere's). **"What we know of body is
  only that it necessarily receives three dimensions; its specific quiddity is unknown to us"** — innate reason cannot say
  whether bodies are alike.
- **Razi's own argument ("what we have achieved")**: bodies are equal in extension (ḥajmiyya) and differ in individuation.
  If they differ in quiddity, extension ≠ the differentiators; then either (a) differentiators are the essences and extension
  inheres in them, (b) extension is the essence and differentiators are its attributes, or (c) neither qualifies the other.
  (a) fails: if the differentiators are in space they would have extension (contradiction); if not, a thing that must be in
  a place cannot inhere in what cannot be (self-evident). (b) gives the thesis: self-standing extensions alike in quiddity
  can have no different necessary concomitants → **whatever is possible for one body is possible for all**: the sphere could
  shed its sphere-attributes and take earth's. (c) is stubbornness (we know fire and earth differ in *their own* states), and
  would still give the thesis. → "a strong, clear proof that bodies are alike in their whole quiddity".
- Opponents' 3 arguments: (1) if alike, individuation is extra → individuations share "being individuation" → regress;
  (2) each body's individuation: by nothing / by itself (shared, cannot differentiate) / by a separate agent (equal bodies →
  unpreponderated) / by receptacle-predisposition (then bodies would be inhering attributes, not self-standing); (3) same for
  specific attributes. *Answers*: (1) body-quiddity and individuation, once joined, each individuates the other — regress
  cut; (2) **individuation may be privative**, needing no cause; (3) philosophers: each state is prepared by a prior state of
  the matter; **mutakallimūn: a free agent may specify some essences with some attributes *without* a preponderator**.
