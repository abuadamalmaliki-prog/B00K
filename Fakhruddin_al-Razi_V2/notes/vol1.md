# Notes — Volume 1 (al-Juzʾ al-Awwal): proofs of God's existence & necessary being

## L1–247 Editor's introduction (al-Saqqā), not Razi
- 9 volumes per the mss: (1) proofs of God's existence/necessity; (2) tawḥīd & tanzīh; (3) positive attributes
  (qādir, ʿālim, ḥayy, samīʿ, baṣīr, mutakallim, bāqī, ḥakīm); (4) ḥudūth/qidam, "secrets of dahr & azal";
  (5) time & place; (6) hylē (hayūlā); (7) higher & lower spirits [the soul]; (8) prophecies; (9) jabr & qadar.
- Editor frames the subject as God's essence & attributes = ʿilm al-ʿaqīda / tawḥīd / kalām / "Islamic philosophy".
- Quotes Hüseyin Atay: Razi absorbed Aristotelian philosophy and was first to bring it fully into kalām
  ("made kalām philosophy and philosophy kalām"); Bayḍāwī's *Ṭawāliʿ* copies the *Muḥaṣṣal*; Iṣfahānī, Ījī follow.
- Bio: Muḥammad b. ʿUmar b. al-Ḥusayn; Ibn al-Khaṭīb; b. Rayy 25 Ramaḍān 543 (Browne: 544); d. Herat 1 Shawwāl 606
  (Qifṭī: Dhū l-Ḥijja 606). Praise from al-Wāfī, Ṭabaqāt al-Shāfiʿiyya, Ibn ʿUnayn, Suyūṭī ("mujaddid" of 6th c.).
  Qifṭī: "Ashʿarī in uṣūl, Shāfiʿī in furūʿ".
- Authentication: Razi's *Sharḥ ʿUyūn al-Ḥikma* refers to *al-Maṭālib* (on individuation/taʿayyun regress);
  Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿa: 3 vols; **Ibn Taymiyya** (*Bayān muwāfaqat ṣarīḥ al-maʿqūl*): "the last thing the Imam wrote, gathering
  the utmost of his learning"; **Ibn al-Qayyim** (*al-Rūḥ*) quotes vol. 7 at length.
- Editor argues vol. 9 (jabr/qadar) is the true last part, not vol. 8: vol. 8 says "the doctrine of jabr is true…
  its full treatment comes in the ninth book"; "fifteen ways prove jabr is true". Vol. 9 was planned in 3 masāʾil but only
  *khalq al-afʿāl* exists; Razi died before writing on **maʿād** and **akhlāq**. A colophon line about Tankalūshā
  (Teukros) "sihr of images" chapter is judged an interpolation.
- Mss: Lâleli 2441 [ل]; Dār al-Kutub 45 [م]; Ṭalʿat 581 [ط/طا]; Ṭalʿat 492; Qawmiyya 1983 [و]; Taymūr 9 [ت];
  Azhar 1961 [ز]; Esad Efendi 1284 [س] (copied 5 Shawwāl 640 from a copy of the author's autograph). Two families:
  (ل, ط581, ط492, س) vs (م, و, ت, ز). Base ms differs per volume.
- L248: Razi titles the work "known in Greek as *Bāthūlūjiyā* (Theologia), among Muslims ʿilm al-kalām or Islamic philosophy".

## L252–280 Razi's opening
- "This is our book in divine science (al-ʿilm al-ilāhī), called in Greek *Bāthūlūjiyā*; arranged as a Muqaddima + books."
- Muqaddima has 4 fuṣūl: (1) this science is the noblest absolutely; (2) can human reason reach certainty (jazm/yaqīn)
  here, or must some questions settle for "the more fitting/likely" (al-awlā wa-l-akhlaq)?; (3) is there one path to these
  holy cognitions or more?; (4) the "knots" (maʿāqid) of the science.

## L284–360 Muqaddima F1: this science is the noblest (4 wujūh)
1. **Nobility of its object** (God's essence & attributes). God is most noble because He is: (i) free of any agent
   and any receptacle (fāʿil/qābil); (ii) absolutely single → no constituent part; (iii) the only necessary-by-itself,
   all else possible and needing Him; (iv) the possible needs its cause *both* at origination *and* in continuance
   (ḥāl al-baqāʾ) — and even its non-existence is caused ("the cause of non-existence is the non-existence of the cause");
   (v) existence is nobler than non-existence; everything else "considered in itself is found non-existent", He alone
   considered in Himself *is* the existent → He alone is *al-Ḥaqq*, all else *bāṭil* / pure perishing (Q 28:88).
   Principle: the nobler the known, the nobler the knowing.
2. **Need/benefit**: the end is felicity. Bodily pleasures are base (animals share and exceed us; they drag the soul
   from the world of holy spirits to bestiality; they are fleeting; all people instinctively revere the abstemious).
   Spiritual felicities are lasting; their head is knowing the First Principle; ignorance of it = eternal loss.
3. **Experiential**: the perfect man finds his joy (ibtihāj) grows exactly as his absorption in these cognitions grows (Q 13:28).
4. **Both worlds**: worldly order needs true sharīʿa, which needs knowing origin & return; otherworldly welfare needs
   knowing God, angels, books, messengers, Last Day. Its master stands at "the last rank of humanity, first of angelhood".

## L362–588 F2: can reason reach certainty here, or only "the more fitting" (al-awlā wa-l-akhlaq)?
Razi reports that "great sages" held the utmost here is *al-awlā wa-l-akhlaq*; decisive certainty may be impossible in
some questions. He lays out four arguments **for** that view, without rebuttal, closing "a sea without shore" — i.e. he
effectively endorses epistemic modesty about the divine *in detail*.
- **Ḥ1 (the self argument)**: one's own self is the most evident object (every knowing presupposes knowing that I
  know, hence knowing myself; the condition is prior to the conditioned). Yet what "I" denotes — the visible frame, a body
  inside it, an attribute of it, or an immaterial substance — is maximally hard. Same with place (Platonists:
  extended void-dimension, *buʿd*; Aristotelians: containing surface), time, and body (atoms vs. infinite divisibility:
  "nothing but perplexity and taking the more fitting"). A fortiori God.
  *Euclid excursus*: Euclid's methods construct only triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, 15-gon; heptagon & nonagon
  need conic sections (angle trisection), 11-/13-gons are beyond him → reason grasps "a little of the much" even in sensibles.
- **Ḥ2 (sight/insight)**: visibles are too faint (motes), too overpowering (the sun's disc, which looks dark at the
  centre with light pouring from its rim), or moderate. Intelligibles likewise: too faint (fine gradations of mixtures,
  alterations), too overpowering (holy substances, above all God — "seventy veils of light"; "glory to Him who veiled
  Himself from minds by the intensity of His manifestness"), moderate. Result: reason can affirm absolute perfection
  and deny every deficiency **in summary (ijmālan)**; detail (tafṣīl) is not within human reason.
- **Ḥ3 (limits of conception)**: all our concepts are (1) sensibles of the 5 senses, (2) inner states (pain, pleasure,
  hunger…), (3) primitive intellectual notions (existence, non-existence, unity, plurality, necessity, possibility,
  impossibility), (4) composites built by imagination (a sea of mercury) or intellect ("partner of God" from "partner" +
  "god"). Judgments are bounded by concepts. God's reality differs from all of these *in every respect* (partial
  agreement would make Him composite). So His reality is not conceivable to creatures; we only conceive perfection/
  deficiency in ourselves, abstract the absolute notion ("the absolute is part of the restricted"), and affirm it of Him
  stripped of creaturely concomitants. Only *ijmāl*.
- **Ḥ4 (direction of inference)**: one moves cause→effect, equal→equal, or effect→cause; only the last is available.
  The human soul is the lowest of the immaterial beings; it ascends cause by cause (Q 53:42; 42:53; God is "the First"
  on the descent, "the Last" on the ascent, Q 57:3). Most souls stay in sensibles; few reach the imaginal; fewer the
  intelligible; most of those dissolve in the lights of the holy spirits — except one rising "from the ginger of Mars
  to the salsabīl of Jupiter to the camphor of Saturn" (astral reading of Q 76) to the holy presence; "a pure drink"
  (Q 76:21) = purification from contingency onto "the threshold of necessity". Q 34:13.

## L590–712 F3: one path to these cognitions or more? → two
**(a) Naẓar/istidlāl** (the "divine sages"): contingency or origination of sensibles + need of the possible for a
preponderator (murajjiḥ) / of the originated for an originator + impossibility of dawr & tasalsul → an eternal
necessary existent.
- **Critique of Avicenna's "way" in *al-Ishārāt*** (proof from existence qua existence, which Avicenna preferred over
  proof from the contingency of others): Razi says it is *not strong*: (1) its "if possible, it needs the necessary"
  branch is itself the inference from a possible's need for a preponderator — no independent route; (2) even granted, it
  leaves open whether the necessary existent is *these bodies*; one must still prove bodies are possible-in-themselves.
  Hence knowledge of the Necessary comes only via examining sensibles.
**(b) Riyāḍa/mujāhada** (spiritual discipline): purifying the heart of all but God, constant dhikr "with the tongue of
the body and of the spirit" → light, unveilings. Three cautions (maqāmāt):
1. **Human rational souls differ in quiddity** (some luminous/divine, some dark) — "argued at length in *Kitāb al-Nafs*"
   (= vol. 7). *Mine analogy*: most mountains lack ores; of those that have them base ores dominate; gold is rare and
   costly; a cave full of gold appears only across vast ages. So not all who practise attain, and none reaches an end
   (God's majesty is infinite). "Painted kohl is not natural kohl." Aristotle: "whoever would seek divine cognitions
   must acquire another nature (fiṭra ukhrā)" = strip intellect of sense, estimation, imagination.
2. The core is emptying the heart; the soul by nature loves the divine presence, the body's pleasures veil it.
3. **Riyāḍa without naẓar is dangerous**: one may take intermediate unveilings for the final goal; reasoning (which
   separates the impossible from the possible) protects. The ideal: perfect in reasoning + perfect in purification +
   innately apt soul. Aristotle (attrib.): "I drank and was not quenched; when I drank from this sea I was quenched for
   ever." "Who has not tasted does not know."
