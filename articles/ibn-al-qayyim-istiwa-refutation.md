# The Thunderbolts Against the Reinterpretation of Istiwā' as Istīlā'

## From *al-Ṣawāʿiq al-Mursalah ʿalā al-Jahmiyyah wa'l-Muʿaṭṭilah* by Imām Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah (d. 751 AH)

### Translated Selections with Commentary

---

## Introduction

One of the most contested points in Islamic theology (*ʿaqīdah*) is the meaning of God's *istiwāʾ* (ascension/rising) upon the Throne (*al-ʿArsh*). The Qurʾān states in seven places — in Sūrah al-Aʿrāf (7:54), Yūnus (10:3), al-Raʿd (13:2), Ṭāhā (20:5), al-Furqān (25:59), al-Sajdah (32:4), and al-Ḥadīd (57:4) — that Allāh *istawā ʿalā al-ʿArsh* ("rose over the Throne").

Those who practiced *taʾwīl* (figurative reinterpretation) claimed that *istawā* here means *istawlā* ("conquered" or "took possession of"), reducing a divine attribute to a mere statement of sovereignty. Imām Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah (691–751 AH) devoted his monumental work *al-Ṣawāʿiq al-Mursalah* ("The Thunderbolts Sent Against the Jahmites and the Negators") to a systematic demolition of such reinterpretation. He declared that the falsehood of interpreting *istiwāʾ* as *istīlāʾ* can be shown from **forty angles** (*min arbaʿīna wajhan*).

Below are the key arguments translated from the *ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm* critical edition, followed by additional strengthening arguments.

---

## Part I: Translated Passages from al-Ṣawāʿiq al-Mursalah

### 1. The Arabic Language Does Not Support This Interpretation

> وكذلك تأويلهم الاستواءَ بالاستيلاء؛ فإن هذا لا تعرفه العرب من لغاتها، ولم يقله أحدٌ من أئمة اللغة. وقد صرَّح أئمةُ اللغة كابن الأعرابي وغيره بأنه لا يُعرَف في اللغة.

**Translation:**

"Likewise, their reinterpretation of *al-istiwāʾ* as *al-istīlāʾ* (conquering/seizing power) — the Arabs do not recognise this in their language, and not a single Imām of Arabic lexicography has said it. The masters of the language, such as Ibn al-Aʿrābī and others, have explicitly stated that this meaning is not known in the [Arabic] language." *(Vol. 1, p. 109)*

### 2. Even If the Word Bore That Meaning, the Grammatical Construction Forbids It

> ولو احتمل ذلك لم يحتمله هذا التركيب؛ فإن استيلاءه سبحانه وغلبته للعرش لم يتأخر عن خلق السماوات والأرض، والعرشُ مخلوقٌ قبل خلْقِها بأكثرَ من خمسين ألف سنةٍ، كما أخبر بذلك الصادق المصدوق فيما صحَّ عنه.

**Translation:**

"And even if the word could bear that meaning [of conquering], this particular construction would not permit it. For His — glorified be He — conquest and dominion over the Throne was not delayed until after the creation of the heavens and the earth. The Throne was created more than fifty thousand years before their creation, as the Truthful One who is believed [i.e. the Prophet ﷺ] informed us in what is authentically reported from him." *(Vol. 1, p. 109)*

**Commentary:** The Qurʾānic phrasing is *"He created the heavens and the earth in six days, **then** rose over the Throne"* (ثم استوى على العرش). The particle *thumma* ("then") indicates sequence. If *istawā* meant *istawlā* (conquered/dominated), it would absurdly imply that God did not have dominion over the Throne until after He created the heavens and the earth — despite the Throne existing long before creation.

### 3. The Consistent Construction "Istawā ʿalā" Has Only One Meaning

> فالاستواء في هذا التركيب نَصٌّ لا يحتمل غيرَ معناه... واستوى على كذا أي: إذا ارتفع عليه وعلا عليه. لا تعرف العربُ غيرَ هذا.

**Translation:**

"*Istiwāʾ* in this construction [i.e. *istawā ʿalā* — with the preposition *ʿalā*] is a definitive text (*naṣṣ*) that does not admit any other meaning... *Istawā ʿalā* such-and-such means: he rose upon it and ascended over it. The Arabs know nothing other than this." *(Vol. 1, p. 37)*

**Commentary:** Ibn al-Qayyim draws a critical linguistic distinction. The verb *istawā* in absolute form (*mutlaq*) can carry several meanings: completion (*istawā* = matured, as in Q. 28:14), equality (*istawā wa-kadhā* = equalled), and directed ascent (*istawā ilā* = turned towards ascending). But when constructed with *ʿalā*, it has **one and only one** meaning in Arabic: rising above and being elevated upon something. The reinterpreters' error is conflating the absolute meanings with the *ʿalā*-construction meaning.

### 4. The Deception of Multiplying Meanings

> فيقال لهذا الجاهل الظالم الفاتن المفتون: ويلَك! ما ذنب الموحِّد الذي سمَّيتَه أنت وأصحابك مُشبِّهًا، وقد قال لك نفس ما قال الله... وأمَّا قولك: «للعرش سبعة معانٍ أو نحوها، وللاستواء خمسة معانٍ». فتلبيسٌ منك... فإن الاستواء المُعدَّى بأداة «على» ليس له إلَّا معنًى واحد.

**Translation:**

"It is said to this ignorant, oppressive, tempting and tempted person: Woe to you! What is the sin of the monotheist whom you and your companions call an 'anthropomorphist' (*mushabbih*), when he merely told you what God Himself said? ... As for your claim that 'the Throne has seven or so meanings, and *istiwāʾ* has five meanings' — this is deception on your part... For *istiwāʾ* when made transitive by the preposition *ʿalā* has only one meaning." *(Vol. 1, p. 36)*

### 5. The Reinterpretation Destroys the Distinction Between the Throne and Everything Else

> ولو كان الله مستويًا على العرش بمعنى الاستيلاء وهو سبحانه مستولٍ على الأشياء كلها؛ كان مستويًا على العرش وعلى الأرض وعلى السماء وعلى الحشوش والأقذار

**Translation:**

"If God were *mustawī* upon the Throne in the sense of *istīlāʾ* (seizing power), and He — glorified be He — has dominion over all things, then He would be [equally] *mustawī* upon the Throne, upon the earth, upon the sky, and upon the latrines and filth — because He is powerful over all things and has dominion over them all. And since He is powerful over all things, and no Muslim would permit saying 'God is *mustawī* upon the latrines,' then it is impermissible for *istiwāʾ* upon the Throne to mean *istīlāʾ*." *(Vol. 2, p. 840)*

**Commentary:** This is one of the most powerful arguments. If *istawā ʿalā al-ʿArsh* means "conquered/dominated the Throne," then by logical necessity the same would apply to every other created thing — the earth, the toilets, the depths of Hell — since God's dominion (*qudrah*) encompasses everything. But the Qurʾān **singles out** the Throne for *istiwāʾ*, proving that it carries a specific meaning that applies to the Throne in particular, not a universal attribute like dominion.

### 6. The Consistency of Qurʾānic Wording Forbids Reinterpretation

> ومثال ذلك اطِّراد قوله: ﴿اَلرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى اَلْعَرْشِ اِسْتَوى﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ اَسْتَوَى عَلَى اَلْعَرْشِ﴾ في جميع موارده من أولها إلى آخرها على هذا اللفظ، فتأويله بـ «استولى» باطلٌ. وإنما كان يصح أنْ لو كان أكثَرُ مجيئه بلفظ «استولى»، ثم يخرج موضع عن نظائره، ويرد بلفظ «استوى»، فهذا كان يصح تأويله بـ «استولى».

**Translation:**

"An example of this is the consistent recurrence of His saying: *'The Most Merciful rose over the Throne'* and *'then He rose over the Throne'* — in all its occurrences, from first to last, using this [same] wording. Its reinterpretation as *istawlā* (conquered) is therefore void. It would only be valid if the majority of its occurrences used the wording *istawlā*, and then one passage departed from its counterparts and came with the wording *istawā* — in that case its reinterpretation as *istawlā* would be sound. So pay attention to this point, and make it a rule for what may and may not be reinterpreted in a speaker's speech." *(Vol. 1, p. 176)*

**Commentary:** This is a masterful hermeneutical principle: when a speaker uses a word **consistently** across every occurrence, it is impossible to claim they meant something other than the apparent meaning. If God had used *istawlā* in six places and *istawā* in one, there would be grounds for interpreting the exception by the rule. But the Qurʾān uses *istawā* in **every single instance** — seven times — and never once says *istawlā*. This renders the reinterpretation linguistically and methodologically bankrupt.

### 7. The Reinterpreters Fall Into What They Fled

> كحال الذين تأوَّلوا نصوص العلو والفوقية والاستواء فرارًا من التحيز والحصر، ثم قالوا: هو في كل مكانٍ بذاته. فنزهوه عن استوائه على عرشه ومباينته لخلقه، وجعلوه في أجواف البيوت والآبار والأواني والأمكنة التي يُرغَب عن ذكرها.

**Translation:**

"Like those who reinterpreted the texts of [God's] transcendence (*ʿuluww*), aboveness (*fawqiyyah*), and rising (*istiwāʾ*), fleeing from [the supposed implication of] spatial confinement and limitation — then they said: 'He is in every place by His essence.' So they declared Him above rising over His Throne and being distinct from His creation, yet placed Him inside the bellies of houses, wells, vessels, and places one refrains from mentioning." *(Vol. 1, p. 69)*

## Part II: Additional Arguments Strengthening the Case Against Taʾwīl of Istiwāʾ

Beyond Ibn al-Qayyim's arguments, the following points reinforce the case:

### 8. The Argument from Poetic Evidence Claimed for Istīlāʾ

The only Arabic verse ever cited to support *istawā* meaning *istawlā* is:

> *qad istawā Bishr ʿalā al-ʿIrāq / min ghayri sayfin wa damin muhrāq*
> ("Bishr has *istawā* over Iraq / without sword or blood shed")

This line is attributed to al-Akhṭal, but:

1. **Its attribution is unverified.** Major scholars of Arabic language and poetry do not authenticate it as belonging to al-Akhṭal's collected works (*dīwān*). Ibn al-Aʿrābī, one of the greatest Arab lexicographers, denied that *istawā ʿalā* means *istawlā*, and this verse — even if authentic — would be a lone counter-example against the unanimous testimony of the lexicons.

2. **Even if authentic, it proves the opposite.** The verse says Bishr *istawā ʿalā al-ʿIrāq* — Bishr physically occupied, sat upon, and presided over Iraq (i.e., he took his seat of governance there). It does not mean he merely "conquered" Iraq from a distance. If anything, the verse supports the meaning of physical elevation and positioning, not abstract domination.

3. **A single anomalous usage cannot override seven Qurʾānic verses.** Even in standard Arabic philology, a *shādhdh* (anomalous) usage does not define the word. One cannot take a possible metaphorical usage in one contested line of poetry and use it to override the clear, consistent Qurʾānic usage repeated seven times.

### 9. The Argument from the Salaf's Unanimous Understanding

Not a single Companion, nor any of the Tābiʿūn, nor any of the four Imāms, nor any early exegete interpreted *istawā ʿalā al-ʿArsh* as *istawlā*. The well-known statements include:

- **Imām Mālik** (d. 179 AH): "Istiwāʾ is known (*maʿlūm*), its modality is unknown (*majhūl*), faith in it is obligatory, and asking about it is an innovation (*bidʿah*)."
- **Rabīʿah** (Mālik's teacher): Similar wording.
- **Umm Salamah** (the Prophet's wife, raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā): A similar narration is attributed to her.

The phrase "istiwāʾ is known" (*al-istiwāʾ maʿlūm*) means its meaning — rising — is clear and established. They did not say "istiwāʾ is ambiguous" or "could mean istīlāʾ." They affirmed its meaning while consigning its modality (*kayf*) to God.

### 10. The Argument from Semantic Redundancy

If *istawā ʿalā al-ʿArsh* means "dominated/conquered the Throne," the verse becomes meaninglessly redundant. Allāh's dominion (*mulk*) and power (*qudrah*) over all things is already established by dozens of clear verses: *"To Him belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth"* (Q. 2:107), *"God has power over all things"* (Q. 2:20), etc. Why would the Qurʾān need to state — seven separate times — that God "conquered" His own Throne, which was never out of His control? This makes the verse vacuous and without benefit (*fāʾidah*), and the speech of Allāh is far above purposelessness.

### 11. The Argument from the Particle *thumma* ("then")

In five of the seven verses, the phrasing is: *"He created the heavens and the earth in six days, **thumma** (then) He rose over the Throne."* The particle *thumma* denotes sequential ordering. This is coherent if *istiwāʾ* means a real rising that occurred after creation — God rose over the Throne after completing the act of creation. But if it means *istīlāʾ* (dominion), the verse would absurdly imply:

- God did **not** have dominion over the Throne **before** He created the heavens and the earth.
- His sovereignty over the Throne only commenced **after** creation.

This contradicts the foundational creed that God's sovereignty is eternal and unconditioned.

### 12. The Argument from Contextual Coherence

The attribute of *istiwāʾ* is mentioned alongside other attributes of transcendence (*ʿuluww*): angels ascending **to** Him (Q. 70:4), good words rising **to** Him (Q. 35:10), His being above (*fawqa*) His servants (Q. 6:18), the Prophet ﷺ being taken up **to** Him on the Night Journey (Q. 53:8-9), hands being raised **upward** in supplication. All these attributes form a coherent picture: God is above His creation, distinct from it, risen over the Throne. Replacing *istiwāʾ* with *istīlāʾ* shatters this coherence and isolates the verse from its entire Qurʾānic and prophetic context.

### 13. The Argument from Fiṭrah (Natural Disposition)

Every human being who supplicates to God instinctively looks and gestures upward. No one directs their heart downward or sideways when calling upon God. This is the natural disposition (*fiṭrah*) that God implanted in creation. As Ibn al-Qayyim notes, quoting earlier scholars: "We see all Muslims raising their hands towards the sky when they supplicate, because Allāh is risen over the Throne which is above the heavens. Were God not upon the Throne, they would not raise their hands towards it, just as they do not lower them towards the earth when supplicating."

This universal behaviour — across all nations, all eras, all cultures — is an empirical proof that the human soul recognises the reality of God's transcendence and elevation.

---

## Conclusion

Ibn al-Qayyim's refutation of the *istīlāʾ* interpretation rests on converging lines of evidence:

1. **Linguistic:** The construction *istawā ʿalā* in Arabic means only one thing — rising above and being elevated upon. No reliable lexicographer supports *istīlāʾ* as its meaning.
2. **Textual:** The Qurʾān uses this phrasing seven times consistently, never once using *istawlā*. Consistent usage bars reinterpretation.
3. **Logical:** If *istiwāʾ* meant *istīlāʾ*, there would be no distinction between the Throne and any other object, rendering the verse meaningless.
4. **Temporal:** The particle *thumma* implies sequence, which is coherent for a real rising but absurd for eternal dominion.
5. **Consensual:** Not a single Companion, Tābiʿī, or early Imām interpreted it as *istīlāʾ*.
6. **Dispositional:** The universal human instinct to direct supplication upward testifies to the reality the verse affirms.

The reinterpretation of *istiwāʾ* as *istīlāʾ* is not a defensible scholarly position — it is, as Ibn al-Qayyim puts it, "pure fabrication, not a truthful report of how the people of the [Arabic] language used the word" (*inshāʾun maḥḍ lā ikhbārun ṣādiq ʿan istiʿmāli ahl al-lughah*).
