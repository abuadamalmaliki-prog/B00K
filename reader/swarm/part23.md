# Part 23 (ج1/ص417 – ج1/ص433)

This part is almost entirely about الإيمان: its definition, whether it increases and decreases, the Murji'a, how إسلام and إيمان differ, and الاستثناء في الإيمان. It has no direct discussion of the divine attributes. The two items below only touch the question indirectly.

## Evidence
1. [ج1/ص418] speaker: quoting Ibn Taymiyya (endorsed; quoted at length with approval)
   kind: other
   quote: «وأما الجهمية فهو واحد عندهم لا يقبل التعدد فيثبتون واحدا لا حقيقة له، كما قالوا مثل ذلك في وحدانية الرب ﷿ ووحدانية صفاته عند من أثبتها منهم.»
   note: He criticises those who reduce the attributes to one undifferentiated thing "that has no reality" (لا حقيقة له). This implies the attributes have distinct, real content. It is an aside within the إيمان discussion.

2. [ج1/ص432–433] speaker: quoting Ibn Taymiyya (reported, with a critical framing)
   kind: other
   quote: «قالوا يحب في أزله من كان كافرا إذا علم أنه يموت مؤمنا ... يعني ما زال الله يريد أن يثيب هؤلاء بعد إيمانهم ويعاقب إبليس بعد كفره، وهذا معنى صحيح ... وعند هؤلاء لا يرضى عن أحد بعد أن كان ساخطا عليه»
   note: This reports the Kullabi/kalam position behind الموافاة, which reads God's love, pleasure and anger (المحبة، الرضا، السخط) as eternal will (إرادة). Ibn Taymiyya accepts only the "will" reading as a correct meaning. The phrase «وعند هؤلاء» marks the denial that God becomes pleased after being angry as their view, not his. So love, pleasure and anger are implicitly treated as real, distinct attributes. The point is indirect and incidental.

## Strongest item in this part
None decisive. Item 2 is the closest: it implicitly treats المحبة, الرضا and السخط as more than eternal will. Even so, it is incidental to the discussion of الاستثناء.
