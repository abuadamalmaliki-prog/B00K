# Part 46 (ج2/ص382 – ج2/ص399)

Topics in this part: why the Companions are preferred (closing quote from إعلام الموقعين), keeping out of the disputes among the Companions, the Tabi'un and the generation after them, the karamat of the awliya', and whether humans or angels are superior. Nothing here deals with the divine attributes directly. The items below are marginal: they bear only on how the author uses the words معنى and تأويل.

## Evidence
1. [ج2/ص382–383] speaker: quoting Ibn al-Qayyim (إعلام الموقعين) (endorsed: the author closes it with «وبالله التوفيق» and builds his poem on it)
   kind: defines-terms
   quote: «والمعاني الصحيحة مركوزة في فطرهم وعقولهم ... فليس في حقهم إلا أمران: أحدهما قال الله تعالى كذا وقال رسوله كذا، والثاني معناه كذا وكذا، وهم أسعد الناس بهاتين المقدمتين»
   note: This is general hermeneutics, not about the attributes. It treats the Companions' authority as resting on two things: the transmitted text and knowing its meaning («معناه كذا»). On this view the meaning of revealed texts can be known and the Salaf knew it. Weak and indirect evidence.

2. [ج2/ص382، ص383] speaker: quoting Ibn al-Qayyim (p382) / author (p383)
   kind: defines-terms
   quote: «وقد شاهدوا التنزيل، وعرفوا التأويل» (p382); «وعلموا التنزيل والتأويل وآدابه» (p383, author's commentary on the verse)
   note: Here تأويل is used in a positive sense: the Companions' knowledge of what the Qur'an means. It is not the kalam sense of turning a word away from its apparent meaning. This is relevant only for how the author uses the term.

3. [ج2/ص392] speaker: author
   kind: other
   quote: «وهو الولي العارف بالله وصفاته حسب ما يمكن»
   note: In defining the wali, he says the wali knows God and His attributes "as far as is possible". So some knowledge of the attributes is possible, but with a limit. This is marginal and could be read either way.

## Strongest item in this part
none. Item 1 is the closest: an endorsed quotation saying the Salaf knew the meaning («معناه كذا») of the texts. It is general hermeneutics and does not mention the attributes.
